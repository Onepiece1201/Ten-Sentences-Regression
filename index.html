Does Study Time Predict Students’ Final Mathematics Grades?

Author

Kuo Wang

Published

November 18, 2026

Abstract
This study uses linear regression to examine whether study time predicts students’ final mathematics grades.

Introduction

Academic performance is an important indicator of students’ educational success, and the amount of time students spend studying may influence their final grades.

This study uses simple linear regression to examine whether study time predicts final mathematics grades among Portuguese secondary school students.

Literature Review

Previous research by Cortez and Silva (2008) examined how student characteristics and educational factors could be used to predict academic performance.

Their study used data from Portuguese secondary school students and explored the role of different factors in predicting final grades, providing a foundation for the regression analysis in this paper.

Method

The dataset contains information from 395 Portuguese secondary school students, including their reported study time and final mathematics grades.

A simple linear regression model was fitted using study time as the predictor variable and final mathematics grade (G3) as the outcome variable.

Results

The linear regression analysis showed a positive relationship between study time and final mathematics grades (b = 0.534, SE = 0.274, p = 0.052).

However, this relationship was not statistically significant at the .05 level, and the model explained approximately 1.0% of the variance in final grades.

import seaborn as sns

plt.show()

Discussion

The findings suggest that students who report longer study times tend to have slightly higher final mathematics grades, although the relationship was not statistically significant.

Because study time explained only a small proportion of the variance in grades, other factors may play a more important role in predicting academic performance.
